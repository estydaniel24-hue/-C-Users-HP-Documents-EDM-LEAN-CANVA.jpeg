# EDM LEAN CANVA

## Scope and starting assumption

This note treats the current canvas as a first draft, not a finished answer. We did not find an existing source canvas file in `/office-hours/EDM LEAN CANVA` in the repo, so this is a working challenge-and-reframe pass based on the brief, not a claim that the initial draft is validated.

## Original canvas points (draft, unvalidated)

The starting draft appears to be: an EDM-related product or business canvas with some implicit assumptions about a problem, a customer, and a value proposition. At this stage, those are too underspecified to be considered true without evidence.

The main risk is that the canvas is still describing a broad category rather than a concrete opportunity.

## Standard forcing questions

### 1) Who exactly has this problem?

Not enough: "EDM fans / event organizers / nightlife businesses" is far too broad.

A real customer segment needs to be specific:
- local EDM promoters running 1–12 events per month
- independent festival organizers
- venue operators who need better scheduling and audience conversion
- artists or collectives managing bookings and community growth
- brands trying to activate EDM audiences in a local market

Without naming the buyer and user, we cannot know who is paying, who is using the product, and who has the pain.

### 2) How do we know this is a real problem?

We do not yet have evidence for:
- frequency of the pain
- severity of the pain
- current workaround cost
- willingness to pay
- churn or frustration with current tools

Possible evidence we need:
- interviews with 10–20 local EDM promoters and venue operators
- observed manual work in event planning and audience acquisition
- tool stack analysis: spreadsheets, WhatsApp groups, Instagram DMs, Eventbrite, Google Sheets, etc.
- explicit willingness-to-pay statements

If the problem is real, the customer should describe a recurring, costly, irritating workflow they are trying to solve today.

### 3) What happens if this product does not exist?

This is a crucial test.

If nothing changes, then:
- promoters keep using fragmented tools and manual messaging
- event organizers lose time and repeat work
- ticket sales and audience conversion remain inconsistent
- venue and artist coordination is fragmented
- growth is driven by hustle, not process

This helps us find whether the problem is merely inconvenient or operationally expensive.

### 4) Who else is already solving it?

Likely competitors or adjacent solutions include:
- ticketing platforms
- event management tools
- CRM and marketing tools for event businesses
- community-building platforms
- promoter operations software
- social scheduling / campaign tools
- artist management and booking tools

The question is not "is there competition?" but "what exact job-to-be-done are they solving, and where are they weak?"

If we cannot explain why a customer would switch, we do not yet have a defensible wedge.

### 5) What would make a real customer say no?

This is the most valuable pushback.

A strong customer would likely say no if:
- the product is too broad and not solving one painful workflow
- the customer already has a workaround and does not see enough ROI
- the product is too expensive compared with their current tools
- it adds complexity to an already chaotic event operation
- it does not integrate with the channels they already use
- it is built for fans, not for the person making the event happen

If we cannot name the reasons for rejection, the current value proposition is probably too vague.

### 6) What are we assuming without evidence?

At least these assumptions need validation:
- EDM is a strong enough market to warrant a dedicated product
- a specific customer segment exists in enough volume
- they have a repeatable problem, not a one-off need
- they will pay for software and not just use free tools
- there is an unmet gap beyond ticketing and marketing tools
- the product is more valuable than a custom spreadsheet or manual process

These are all hypotheses. They are not facts.

## Pushback on vague or unproven claims

### Problem is vague
The phrase "EDM needs better tools" is not a product problem. It is a category statement.

We need a problem statement like:
- local EDM promoters cannot turn audience interest into attendance because they are juggling tickets, messaging, artist coordination, and venue logistics in disconnected tools
- independent event operators do not have a lightweight way to manage artist, venue, and attendee coordination across multiple event nights

### Customer is vague
"EDM community" is too broad.

The real customer is likely a person with operational responsibility and budget authority, not a fandom segment.

Examples:
- promoter
- venue manager
- event producer
- artist collective lead
- local nightlife operator

### Value proposition is vague
"Better EDM experiences" is not a strong value prop.

A credible value proposition must say what gets better and for whom:
- fewer manual follow-ups
- higher ticket conversion
- easier artist and venue coordination
- better audience segmentation and retention
- less spammy outreach and more repeat attendees

## Reframed problem/customer/value statement

### Better-framed problem
Independent EDM event organizers and local promoters struggle to run events efficiently because they rely on fragmented tools for ticketing, promotion, guest list management, artist coordination, and venue communication. The result is lost time, missed sales, operational chaos, and inconsistent attendee experiences.

### Better-framed customer
Small to mid-sized independent EDM promoters, venue operators, and event producers who run recurring nightlife or music events and need to coordinate sales, audiences, and logistics without a full-time operations team.

### Better-framed value proposition
A lightweight operating system for EDM event businesses that centralizes promotion, ticketing, guest list workflows, and coordination across artists, venues, and audiences—reducing manual admin and improving attendance conversion.

## What would make this valid in the real world?

Before building, we need evidence for:
- 10–20 customer interviews with real promoters and venue operators
- an explicit workflow they hate doing manually
- current tools they use and why they are insufficient
- whether they are willing to pay monthly or per event
- a clear process to acquire customers and retain them

## Final verdict on the draft

The current canvas is not ready to be treated as a product definition. It reads as a directional idea, not a validated business opportunity.

The real next step is to narrow to one customer and one painful workflow, and then test that problem with direct evidence.

## Recommended next iteration

Use this structure instead of the broad canvas:
- Problem: what exact operational pain are we solving?
- Customer: which specific role and market segment has this pain?
- Workflow: what task is currently painful, manual, or inconsistent?
- Existing alternatives: what tools are they using today?
- Why switch: what value is large enough to justify change?
- Revenue model: who pays, how often, and why?

This is the minimum needed before the business idea becomes repeatable and credible.

## One-line summary

EDM is not the customer; the customer is a local event operator juggling promotion, logistics, and attendance outcomes. The product should solve one clear operational bottleneck for that person, not try to become a broad “EDM platform.”
